**patronage** - the support or influence of a **patron**
## Joseph Haydn (1732-1809)

- **composer**, keyboardist, violinist
- working-class family

Music gave Haydn a path to education and became his cultural currency. He went to work under the wealthy **Esterházy family**.

**Esterháza** - Hungarian equivalent of Versailles

Haydn's compositions would emphasize his best players with solos or highlights of the specific instrument.
- Haydn was on a friendship basis with his players, and his sheet music would typically have their names rather than their instruments.

Prince Nikolaus Esterázy I was a barytone player, so Haydn wrote over 200 pieces for the barytone. 
- Having the patron play the main melody made the them at the musical center.

Haydn goes to London when Nikolaus I Esterházy dies, renegotiates his contract and sells his symphony to patrons or publishers abroad.

Haydn composes for the producer Johann Peter Salomon, and writes twelve new symphonies (**London symphonies**) for new audiences. Money was made for musicians in London in the purchase of concert tickets.
- Mitigating the financial risk of the concert business involved having a subscription model.
