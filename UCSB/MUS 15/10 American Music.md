### Antonín Dvořák (1841-1904)

- Czech nationalist composer born in Prague
- comes to America in 1892 to become director of the National Conservatory of Music in New York City
- composed *Symphony No. 9 in E Minor* " From the New World" Op. 95
- charged by his patron (Jeanette Thurber) to teach students how to compose American music
- interested in Native American and African spiritual music
- wrote "The Real Value of Negro Melodies" (1893), citing black melodies, spirituals, and folk music as American

**spiritual genre** - a type of sacred song created by an d for African Americans that originated in oral tradition
- genre by the early 19th century
### Harry Burleigh (1866-1949)

- singer, composer, arranger, music editor
- win scholarship to study at the National Conservatory of Music with Dvorak
- interested in African American spirituals, becoming close with Dvorak
- Burleigh's singing incorporated into Dvorak's compositions

**melodic contour** - shape of the melody
### Amy Beach (1867-1944)

- American composer and self-taught vituoso pianist in Boston
- wrote "Letter to the Editor" after Dvořák's publication; claims the African population is too small
- an American sound should use English, Scotch, or Irish songs

*Symphony in E Minor, Op. 32* "Gaelic Symphony"
- inspired by English, Irish, and Scottish folk tunes
- first symphony by a woman composer in the United States
- premiered at Boston Symphony in 1869
