## Arnold Schoenberg (1874-1951)

- Austrian **atonal/post-tonal** composer and music theorist
- figure in modernism
- emigrates to the U.S. in 1931 fleeing the Nazis
- proffesor of music at U.C.L.A.

***Five Pieces for Orchestra*** - atonal composition that was poorly recieved piece in 1907

Schoenberg wrote pieces that mosy audiences did not like. He created the **Second Viennese School**, which was a group/cult/philosophy. He taught students and gives music lessons.

**Scandal Concert of 1913*(slap concert; Skandalkonzert)** - concert conducted by Schoenberg hosted by Vienna Concert Society containing music from the Second Viennese School
- riot, public outcry during the concert; concert ended half way through
- producer slapped a concertgoer, sparking brawls
- lawsuits ensued

**The Society for Private Musical Performances in Vienna** - retreat from the concert hall; private music society following Schoenberg
- clear, well-rehersed performances and frequent repetition
- no public critics and corrupting influence
- no applause or demonstrations of dissaproval
- wanted to focus on the piece rather than any individual musicians

Europe in the early 20th century moves from music with patrons to becoming state supported. Radio and court institutions were state supported. The Vienna Court Opera became the Vienna State Opera.

### Andrew Carnegie (1835-1919)

- steel, railroads, philantropist
- patron of the arts to the New York Philharmonic Orchestra, Symphony Orchestra, and Oratorio Society.

Many composers wanted the same kind of treatment as the composers of the past.
## Elizabeth Sprague Coolidge (1864-1953)

- wealthy, patron of the arts
- conservatory trained pianist
- "fairy godmother of chamber music"
- commissions compositions, funds competitions, and gives prizes
- hires ensembles
- creates **Coolidge String Quartet**
- funds the **Coolidge auditorium** in the Library of Congress
- Sprague Hall, Yale School of Music
