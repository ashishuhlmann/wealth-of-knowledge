**phonography** - the process of the writing of sound

**phonograph** - first sound recording device; machine for entertainment and scientific and humanistic study
- invented in 1877 by Thomas Edison
- used for ethongraphic fieldwork in the 1890s (mainly to study folk music)
- mainstream in households by 1895
- comercial record industry established by the 1900s

**Universal Exposition** - annual global fair for technology and culture

Paris 1889
- gallery of machines, phonograph
- textile gallery
- habitation exhibits; Senegal, Hawaii, China, Javanese Village
- Vietnamese musicians
- Pagoda of Angkor

**Berlin Phonogram Archive** - musicology archive established by Carl Stumpf (1848-1936) in 1900 philosphy
- directed by Erich Moritz von Hornbostel (1877-1935) in 1906 chemistry
- Institue of Psychology at the University of Berlin
- comparative musicology and ethnomusicology
- mission to collect traditional music and create theories about the origin and evolution of music
- started with 16,000 recordings from all over the world (limited to a few minutes)
- defined "folk music" from the outside
