**Define musical exoticism. Then, discuss one musical example from lectures that illustrates this**
**concept.**

Musical exoticism is the composition by one culture that attempts to immitate attributes of another. These immitations differ from the authentic culture. At the turn of the century, Tin Pan Alley songs such as Yaaka Hula Hickey Dula (Hawaiian Love Song) threw together Hawaiian themes in attempts at immitating Hawaiian culture. Lyrics often dreamed of a far away paradise, ukeleles, Hawaiian cuisine, Hula dancing, or women as objects of attraction. Songs like Yaaka Hula Hickey Dula also combined nonsensical syllables that " sounded" Hawaiian. Exoticism grew in Tin Pan Alley music with overthrowing of the Hawaiian Monarchy. World fairs such as Chicago's 1893 world fair often contained "exhibits" of other culture and people which only added to the immitation of other cultures.

**In what ways were musical instruments especially central to the early music revival? And, please**

Music revival in the twentieth century centered around a early music nostalgia and a goal to create the past in a way that was fresh and exciting to new audiences of new times. Musical instruments themselves were essential to historically informed performance movements. Creating the original sound involved period instruments, which were either original instruments centuries in age or accurate replicas of early instruments. For example, gut strings are typically on stringed instruments. Francis Galpin was a significant figure of early music rivival. He was an English priest, collector, and organologist who pioneered the crafting of period instruments. One core idea of the revival he believed in was that old instruments needed to be brought out of their musuem cases and be played for everyone to here. His collection of over 600 instruments however, ironically ended up behind glass cases in the Museum of Fine Arts in Boston.

**Define and discuss “historically informed performance” (“HIP”) practice.**

Historically informed performance (HIP) is the performance of historical music through period instruments. These instruments are either originals dating back hundreds of years or are recreations of old instruments mimicking the way they would have sounded back then. Groups such as Piffaro or the Orchestra of the Age of Enlightenment aim to make the past exciting for new audiences today. Groups like these formed as a result of collegium musicum, or small amateur/proffessional music gatherings. HIP gained a lot of popularity by the end of the 20th century, and universities began to offer early music degrees by the 1990s. David Munrow brought HIP through the Early Music Consort of London to the TV screens of many viewers with his show detailing the description and performance of early music. His interest peaked in the folk music of South America, and Munrow's playing became such a cultural icon that it made its way into space on the Golden Record. HIP plays music of the past in a way that performers think it sounded like when it was new.

The industrial revolution into the 19th century led to an influx of people in to cities and pushed certain education that made amateur choral singing more accessible. Musical literacy was promoted among many other qualities of the educated, and companies such as Novello & Co., cheap sheet music became available to public consumers. The 19th century is also when the tonic sol-fa singing method was invented, and this led to the formation amateur choral societies and national festivals. These include societies such as the Handel and Haydn Society or Lower Rhine music festival. As people had more time and practice in singing capability, a shift from amateurism to proffessionalism was demonstrated by the growing proffessional member percentage of these communities during this time. Fisk University's Jubilee Singers also shows how Reconstruction and scientific curiuosities in spirituals and folk music also added to the growth of choral communities. Classics from George Handel such as *Messiah* were preformed along with newer compositions from Johanne Brahms as vocal scores became more common.
