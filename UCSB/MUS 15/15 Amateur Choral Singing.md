## George Handel (1685-1759)

- opera and anthem composer known for gigs
- composed the "Hallelujah" chorus in *Messiah* (**oratorio**)

**opera**
- **libretto** - words or poetry by a **librettist**
- divided into acts
- singers or solo chorus, orchestral players, and dancers
- costumes and sets
- hightented speech

**oratorio**
- libretto based on Biblical material (not part of service or liturgy)
- words set to a score by a composer
- two or three parts (two or three hours)
- singers and orchestral players without costumes and sets
- aria and recital sections
## Industrial Revolution (1760-1840)

- mechanized labor; factories; steam engine trains
- influx of people into cities; shift from agricultural societies to industrial societies
- new working class subject to educational projects
- new skills like musical literacy pushed
- tonic sol-fa singing method invented
- increase in access to musical materials like cheap sheet music
- choral societies and national festivals
### Vincent Novello (1781-1861)

- English organist, composer, music publisher
- founded Novello & Co. in 1811 taken over by son Joseph Alfred Novello (1810-1896)
- bass singer; takes over company in 1829
- introducees inexpensive, affordable sheet music to public consumers
## 19th Century Shifts

- **dilettantism** (amateurism) to professionalism
- amateurs to virtuosi

Professional orchestras are formed in the 19th century.

**Handel and Haydn Society** - performance group in Boston
- founded in 1815
- American chorus and period insturment orchestra
- longest-serving organization of its kind in the U.S.
- founded as an oratorio society to promote the love of good music

**Lower Rhine Music Festival**
- founded in 1818
- three-day festival
- becomes a monster ensemble by the 1850s
