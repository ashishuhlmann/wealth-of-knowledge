## 4/13/26

## Goals

You are here to play back to the writer how you read the paper. Share what you got from it, what you find interesting, confusing, and where you wanted to know more.
- You are not an editor or teacher.
### Circumstances

Glance through the paper first and get an idea of whether you will deal with overall content and purpose, shape and flow, or local matters of paragraph structure, sentence style, and correctness.
## Commenting

Leave comments in the margins and in a note at the end.
- Use specific full statements.
- Explain what you mean.
- Let the writer know where a problem is.
- Praise as much as you critique, and have very specfic praises as well.
- Instead of telling the writer what to do, suggest alternative methods.
### Examples

1. Play back your way of understanding the writing:
- This seems to be the real focus of the paper, the issue you seem most interested in.
- So you're saying that you really weren't interested in her romantically?
2. Temper your criticisms:
- This sentence is a bit hard to follow.
- I'm not sure this paragraph is necessary.
3. Offer advice:
- It might help to add an example here.
- Maybe save this sentence for the end of the paper.
4. Ask questions, especially real questions:
- What else were you feeling at the time?
- What kind of friend? Would it help to say?
- Do you need this opening sentence?
- In what ways were you "a daddy's little girl"?
5. Explain and follow up on your initial comments:
- You might present this episode first. This way we can see what you mean when you say that he was always too busy.
- How did you react? Did you cry or yell? Did you walk away?
- This makes her sound cold and calculating. Is that what you want?
6. Offer some praise, and then explain to the writer why the writing works:
- Good opening paragraph. You've got my attention.
- Good detail. It tells me a lot about the place.
- I like the descriptions you provide-for instance, about your grandmother cooking, at the bottom of page 1; about her house. in the middle of page 2; and about how she said her rosary at night: "quick but almost pleading, like crying without tears."
