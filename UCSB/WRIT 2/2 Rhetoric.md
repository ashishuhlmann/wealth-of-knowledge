## 4/7/26

Most of our actions are persuasive in nature.

**rhetoric** - what allows you to write appropriately for a given situation
- **rhetor** - author trying to persuade
- determined by the expectations of the **audience**
## The Rhetorical Situation

1. **exigence** - circumstance or condition that invites a response
- something that needs to be done
2. **audience** - those who are the recipients of the rhetorical message
- should be able to respond to the exigence
3. **constraints** - constrain the decision and action needed to modify exigence
- related to how the rhetoric is presented
## The Argument

**argument** - what the rhetor wants you to believe or do

1. **logos** - arguments from logic and reason
- statistics or anecdotes
2. **pathos** - emotional appeal 
- can persuade in a short amount of time
3. **ethos** - credibility of the rhetor
- writing tone and accuracy of information
## Your Questions

- Does the rhetoric address the problem it claims to address?
- Is the rhetoric targeted at an audience who has the power to make change?
- Are the appeals appropriate to the audience?
- Does the rhetor give enough information to make an informed decision?
- Does the rhetoric attempt to manipulate in any way (by giving incomplete information or abusing the audience's emotions)?
- What other sub-claims do you have to accept to understand the rhetor's main claim?
- What possible negative effects might come from this rhetoric?
