## Definition of Genre

**Genres** are more than filling in blanks to a set of rules or structure.

Genres are patterns of how people respond to reoccuring situations.
- Genres develope as writers respond appropriately to situations that happen again.

"Genres have the power to help or hurt human interaction, to ease communication or to deceive, to enable someone to speak or to discourage someone from saying something different" (*Writing* 1)

When writing in a certain genre, we must adapt or rhetoric to the specific situation that we are responding to.
- This is why two texts of the same genre may look completely different.
- Following genre standard **conventions** is what makes writing efficient.

"When people write, they draw on the genres they know, their own context of genres, to help construct their rhetorical action. If they encounter a situation new to them, it is the genres they have acquired in the past that they can use to shape their new action. Every genre they acquire, then, expands their genre repertoire and simultaneously shapes how they might view new situations."
(Devitt, *Writing* 203)
