## 3/30/26

**electric charge** - property of matter
- comes as positive or negative
- conserved
- **quantized**

The **electric force** between a pair of charges is
$$
\textbf{F}=k\frac{q_{1}q_{2}}{r^{2}}\hat{\textbf{r}}
$$
- $k\approx8.988\cdot10^{9}\frac{\text{Nm}^{2}}{\text{C}^{2}}$ is **Coloumb's constant**
- like gravity, but without a negative sign
- positive radial force for like charges
- negative radial force for opposite charges

**law of superposition** - the electric force on a charge from multiple other charges is the vector sum of all the individual electric forces

The **electrostatic potential energy** is
$$
U=k\frac{q_{1}q_{2}}{r}
$$
- for multiple discrete charges, simply sum up the potential for each pair

Coloumb's constant is
$$
k=\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_{0}}
$$
- $\epsilon_{0}\approx8.854\cdot10^{-12}\frac{\text{C}^{2}}{\text{Nm}^{2}}$ is the **permitivity of free space**

**electric field** - vector field of electric force per unit charge
- real physical property of space
- for a discrete set of charges, it is
$$
\textbf{E}=k\sum_{i}\frac{q_{i}}{r_{i}^{2}}\hat{\textbf{r}}_{i}
$$
- for a continuous distribution of charge, it is
$$
\textbf{E}=k\int_{V}\frac{\rho}{r^{2}}\hat{\textbf{r}} dV
$$
- The force on a charge in an electric field is simply $\textbf{F}=q\textbf{E}$.

**voltage** - scalar field potential per unit charge
$$
-\nabla V=\textbf{E}
$$
**electric flux** - electric field dot area vector
$$
\phi_{E}=\int_{S}\textbf{E}\cdot d\textbf{A}
$$
