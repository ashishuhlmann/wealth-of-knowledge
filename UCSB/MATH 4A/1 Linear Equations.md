## Fundamentals

**Linear Equation** - any mathematical equation with variables with exponents no greater than one
- A linear equation in '$n$ variables', $x_1, x_2, ... x_n$ is an equation that can be written as $$a_1x_1+a_2x_2+...+a_nx_n=b$$ where $a_i$ and $b$ are either real numbers *or* complex numbers.
### Example
$$
\begin{align}
2x_1+4x_2+10&=x_2 \\
2x_1+3x_2&=-10 \\
\end{align}
$$
- **systems of linear equations** or **linear system** - a collection of linear equations that involve the same variables
### Example
$$
\begin{align}
2x_1-x_2+x_3&=8 \\
x_1\phantom{--}-4x_3&=-1 \\
\end{align}
$$
## Solutions

A **Solution of a Linear System** in $n$ variables is a set of $n$ numbers which when substituted in for the variables, satisfies the system.
- a system of linear equations can either have *a unique solution*, *infinitely many solutions*, or *no solution* independent of dimension
### Example

$$
\begin{align}
x+y=0 \\
3x+3y=0 \\ 
\end{align}
$$
$$
\begin{bmatrix}
x \\
y \\
\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix}
-t \\
t \\
\end{bmatrix}
\phantom{-}\forall t \in R
$$
### Example

$$
\begin{align}
x+2y=0 \\
-2x-4y=3 \\
\end{align}
$$
$$0=3\phantom{-}\#$$
$$\text{no solution}$$
- such a linear solution is said to be **inconsistent**
- *Note: a contradiction symbol is a $\#$
## Geometric Interpretations

Solutions of a system of $n$ linear equations in two variables $x$ and $y$ is the intersection of $n$ lines in the $(x,y)$ plane.
1. unique intersection
2. double line
3. distinct parallel lines

An equation with three or more lines that don't intersect at the same point has *no solution*.
