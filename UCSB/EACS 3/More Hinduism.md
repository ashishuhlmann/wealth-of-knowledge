"Hinduism" term was given by the invaders.

**three margas** or **trimarga** - three paths or three **yogas**
1. **karma-marga** - duty and responsibility
2. **jiñāna-marga** - philosophical insight
3. **bhakti-marga** - faith and devotion

**yoga** - taming ego and channeling its energy
- purpose of yoga to control body, thoughts, and desires
- brings together the true self with the more eternal cosmic self

**ātman** - true self

**Brahman** - cosmic self
- we only see our outside selves and material things rather than deeper internal unity
- liberation from embodied existence

Religious texts tend to focus on violence.

Rather than finite action with infinite consequences (heaven and hell), Hinduism uses proportional consequences through many lives. Hinduism blames personal action, circumstances, and actual conditions to explain the current state rather than any gods or fate.
- Critics of karma argue for that those in poverty or challenged physically and mentally are not at fault for their circumstances, and that this belief maintains social hierarchies for generations
## The Bhagavad Gita

- great war dividing a single clan
### Karma-Marga

Arjuna is overly concerned with how the consequences of his actions will reflect on himself.
- he must fulfill his duty through selfless action

**varna-āśrama-dharma** - moral system of **karma**, rebirth, and **dharma**; generational duty

**karma** - moral cause and effect; action and its consequences; the energy that spins the wheel of rebirth
- only *intentional* action is karmic
- actions fueled by greed, or hatred generate negative karmic consequences while actions fueled by non-attachment generate positive karmic consequences

**saṃsāra** - wheel of existence; everyday world experience

**kali-yuga** - age of injustice and warfare

People have control over their future lives.

**castes** - social groups
1. **daltis** - outcaste
2. **sudra** - labor and service
3. **vaisya** - merchants and capitalists
4. **ksatriya** - government officials
5. **brahmin** - priests and religious leaders

**jati** - subcastes

**asramas** - phases
1. student phase
2. householder phase
3. retiree phase
4. Sannyasa phase
### jñana-marga

It is or people and controlled by the mind.
- if we awake to truth, if we achieve cosmic consciousness, then we see the unity of all things

**avidya** - un-seeing, ignorance
- self is multilayered: body, mind, motivations -> pure spirit

**jiva** - drop, spark
- My jiva has gained wisdom from all my lives.
- pure spirit is reincarnated
- Egos are shaped by past actions even in previous lives.

**purusarthas** - wants and desires
1. **kama** - pleasure
	- Sex is ego-centered and can satisfy physical desires but not deeper needs.
2.  **artha** - acquisitiveness
	- Wealth can be shared and bring comfort, but cannot meet the soul's drive for freedom.
3. **dharma** - service
	- Highest and most noble personal aspirations.
4. **moksa** - liberation from embodiment
## Bhakti-marga

**puja** worship service to gods done at Hindu temples
- Most Hindus would describe Hinduism as monotheistic since all dieties are manifestations of Brahman.
- Hindus choose a personal being or personal **ista-devata** to cherish as one god.
- Focusing on one diety at a time allows them to worship one god.
- reciting of the 108 names of god
### Attributes of God (Thomas Aquinas 1225-1274)

1. incorporeality: being beyond any single form
2. ineffability: inexpressible in words
3. unity: no other god
4. eternity: existed before time began and is eternal, transcending all cycles
5. immutability: perfect and indestructible
6. omnipotence: all-powerful creator, sustainer, and destroyer
7. omniscience: all-knowing
8. goodness: benevolent, fulfillment of the highest spiritual goal

**sanguna Brahman** - Brahman with characteristics
- Brahman as **Krishna** loves the world and asks for love in return.
- Brahman as **Shiva** is known through mystical union.
- Shiva represents the overcoming of opposites.
### Shiva

1. Siva means kindness and benevolence, while Lord Siva is the destroyer
2. aniconic form as **lingam**, patron god of **sannyasi**, those who have given up all sexual desire
3. **ardha-narisvara** - both male and female
4. quiescent but active when in union with divine feminine principle
5. lingam in union with **yoni**
