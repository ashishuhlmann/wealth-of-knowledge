## Emile Durkheim (1858-1917)

- French Jewish scholar; "Father of Sociology"
- lived during rapid industrialization and urbanization and the decline of religion
- came from a family of rabbis
- concerned with the secularizing of society and decline of tools to maintain **social solidarity** or sense of morality and purpose, communal life; people feel alone and alienated, no clear goals

"The whole is larger than the sum of its parts."
- emphasized a "holistic approach" and "social fact"
- believed all religions to be authentic and true
- despite the decline of the supernatural in modern life, we still have powerful religious objects that elicit powerful emotions

**collective effervescence** - collective rituals and gatherings concentrate the religious force and produce the powerful feelings of unity, identification, and social solidarity that are necessary to repeatedly and periodically remind people of their belongingness and social obligations, and to produce a good feeling about being together

**social solidarity** - religious feeling, emotions, and rituals that maintain a sense of community identification and belonging

**sacred objects** - religious feeling channeled into key sacred symbols and objects that encapsulate meaning

**structural-functionalism** - the function of religion is to generate social solidarity, avoid modern anomie; the function of sacred objects, places, symbols, and religious activities like rituals or festivals is to produce and maintain society

## The Totemic Principle

Rreligion is predicated on the distinction between **sacred** and **profane**
- **sacred** - taboo, protected and isolated by prohibitions
- **profane** - must keep at a distance from what is sacred

**Totem** - borrowed from Native American language
- "Collective feelings become conscious of themselves only by settling upon external objects, those very forces could not organize themselves without taking some of their traits from things."

**post colonial awareness** - both a modern (western) and pre-modern view
