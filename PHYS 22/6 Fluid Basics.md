## Pressure

**pressure** - force per unit area
$$
p=\frac{\textbf{F}\cdot\textbf{A}}{\textbf{A}\cdot\textbf{A}}
$$
- $\textbf{A}$ is the **area vector** that is perpendicular to the surface with magnitude of the total area.
- $\textbf{F}$ is the force on the surface.

**bulk modulus** - an object's resistance to uniform compression
$$
B=-V_{0}\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta V}
$$
- $\Delta p$ is the change in pressure
- $\Delta V$ is the change in volume
- $V_{0}$ is the original volume

## Liquids and Gases

**ideal liquid** - incompressible fluid that still moves under shear forces

The pressure going up through a liquid follows the differential equation
$$
\frac{dp}{dy}=-\rho g
$$
This can be written as
$$
p(y)=p_{0}-\rho g y
$$
- $p_{0}$ is the pressure exerted at the surface of the liquid.
- $y$ is the height measured upward where $y=0$ is the surface of the liquid.

**Theorem:** Pascal's principle
- Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted undiminished to every portion of the fluid and to the walls of the containing vessel.

**Theorem:** Archemedes' principle
- A body wholly or partially immersed in a fluid is buoyed up by a force equal in magnitude to the weight of the fluid displaced by the body.

**compression** - ability of a fluid to compress
- liquids are usually treated as incompressible

**steady flow** - pressure, density, and flow velocity are constant at every point
- **streamline** - path a particle traces out during steady flow
- no two streamlines cross over in steady flow

**Bernoulli's equation** 
$$
p+\frac{1}{2}\rho v^{2}+\rho gh=\text{Const.}
$$
## Air Pressure

Since air is compressible, the pressure in a column of air varies as
$$
\frac{dp}{dy}=-g\rho_{0}\frac{p}{p_{0}}
$$
This can be written as
$$
p(y)=p_{0}\exp{(\frac{-g\rho_{0}}{p_{0}}y)}
$$
- $y$ is measured upwards, where $y=0$ is sea level.
- $\rho_{0}$ is the density at sea level.
- $p_{0}$ is the pressure at sea level.
