## 3/24/26

**photometry** - measurement of light intensity

**spectroscopy** - measurement of the light spectrum of an object

**polarimetry** - measurement of light polarization

**index of refraction** - fraction of the speed of light in a material to the speed of light in a vaccum
$$
n=\frac{v}{c}
$$
**Snell's law** - describes the bending of light through different mediums
$$
n_{1}\sin{\theta_{I}}=n_{2}\sin{\theta_{R}}
$$
- both angles are measured from the vertical
- If traveling into a lower index, **total internal reflection** happens at a critical angle when the refracted beam angle is 90 degrees.
$$
\theta_{C}=\sin^{-1}{(\frac{n_{2}}{n_{1}})}
$$

**Fermat's Principle** - the path of light taken through any media minimizes the travel *time*
$$
T=\int_{C}\frac{ds}{v}=\frac{1}{c}\int_{C}nds
$$
- $C$ is measuring along the path of light

**optical path length** - path length times the intex of refraction
$$
S_{O}=\int_{C}nds
$$
## Geometric Components

**prisms** - used to disperse or redirect light

**lenses** - used to focus light
- **optical axis** - straight line passing through the geometric center of a lense and joins the two centers of curvature of its surfaces
- **converging lens** - causes rays to refract toward the optical axis
- **diverging lens** - causes rays to refract away from the optical axis

For thin lenses, the **focal length** is
$$
\frac{1}{f}=\frac{1}{d_{o}}+\frac{1}{d_{i}}
$$
- $d_{o}$ is the distance from the object to the lens
- $d_{i}$ is the distance from the lens to the image
- $f$ is the focal length

**real images** - images formed from converging rays that go through the image

**virtual images** - images formed from diverging rays that don't go through the image
## Telescopes

Refracting telescopes use an **object lens** (converging) and an **eyepiece lens** to compress all incoming rays to pass through the eye's pupil.

**angular magntification** - angular size of an object divided by the angular size of the image
$$
m_{\theta}=-\frac{f_{0}}{f_{e}}
$$
- When $m_{\theta}<0$, the image is inverted.

**image scale** - angular distance between objects in the sky divided by the focal plane distance
$$
s=\frac{\theta}{x}=\frac{1}{f}=\frac{206265''}{f}
$$
**field of view** - angular size of the region on the sky covered by the detector

The **light-gathering power** of an optical telescope is proportional to the area of the objective lens.

Different wavelengths of light have different indicies of refraction and will not all be in focus at the same time. This is called **chromatic aberration**.

**focal ratio** - effective focal length divided by the diameter of the objective lens or primary mirror
- commonly written as (f/8)
## Diffraction

**airy pattern** - bullseye patter of light diffracting through a circular opening
- for wavelength $\lambda$ and lens or mirror diameter $D$, the angular distance between the central maximum and the first diffraction minimum is
$$
\theta=\frac{1.22\lambda}{D}
$$
**Rayleigh criterion** - maximum of on star's diffraction pattern is coincident with the other star's first diffraction minimum

**scintillation** - change in light's wavefront due to the atmnosphere or different mediums
- **seeing** - how much a stellar image is blurred by the atmosphere, or the full width at half maximum (FWHM) of the stellar profile
