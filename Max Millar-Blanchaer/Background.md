## Motivations

*Why study exoplanets?*
- to undestand our origins and the formation of our own solar system
- is part of the ongoing search for Earth-like planets and conditions for life
- study analogs of components in our own solar system
- characterize our own solar system in the context of the universe and our place in the universe
- hot jupyters changed migration theories
- incomplete picture of mass loss from the surfaces of stars

**polarimetry** - measurement and interpretation of the polarization of transverse waves.
- extremely valuable in high contrast imaging
- used to study cloud morphology in exoplanets LUHMAN 16 A and B
- concluded that polarization for LUHMAN 16A could only be explained by banded clouds
- WIRC+Pol project trying to understand clouds in brown dwarfs
- allows deeper sight into heterogeneous exoplanets and characterizations of their surfaces

**polarimetric differential imaging (PDI)** - selecting for certain polarizations to obtain the light from one source
- scattered light off a disk is polarized while direct starlight is not

**angular differential imaging (ADI)** - subtracking images of a target as the night sky rotates together to eliminate a background star
- suffers at disks with low inclination

**Visible Aperture-Maksing Polarimetric Interferometer for Resolving Exoplanetary Structures (VAMPIRES)** - visible light instrument on the **Subaru telescope** (8.5 m)
- **instrumental polarization** - polarization from optical elements
- **crosstalk** - equipment causing blending of polarization states
- aim to use both standard stars and internal sources to calibrate analysis further

**NIRC2 Polarimetry** is a new infrared + polarimetry observing mode on the **Keck II** 10-meter telescope.
- includes two new half-waveplates and a Wollaston prism
