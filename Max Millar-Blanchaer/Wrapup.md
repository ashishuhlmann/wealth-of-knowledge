## 9/6/26

NIRC2 Polarimetry is a new observing mode on the 10-meter Keck II telescope at Mauna Kea in Hawai'i. Polarimetry is useful for a range of exciting science cases, from planet formation to active galactic nuclei. Over this quarter, I spent a lot of time learning the basics of high-contrast imaging and polarimetry, reading through an astronomy textbook and three publications. I built some background in observational astronomy and learned the basics of dark subtraction and flat fielding. I worked these image processing skills in Jupyter Notebooks, before learning all about polarization, Stokes Parameters, and polarimetric differential imaging. Finally, I began working on a translation of a reduction pipeline originally written in Julia. While beginning this project, I also navigated the essence of Git and version control, and spent a lot of time with the Linux command line and Secure Shell (SSH) to connect to remote lab computers and develop this pipeline. The notebook is designed to dark subtract and flat field entire directories of NIRC2 data at once, scanning through FITS headers, making masks, and combining images. I translated and optimized a good portion of the code going through many rounds of debugging and testing, and by the end of the quarter, the program could create a master dark file with a little more work needed for flats and the complete reduction. On May 26th, I also helped to log observing details for half of night during an observation run with NIRC2.
